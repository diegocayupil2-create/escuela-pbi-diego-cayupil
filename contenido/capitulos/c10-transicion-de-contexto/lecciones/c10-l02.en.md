## The idea

When you use a measure inside an iterator (`FILTER`, `SUMX`, `AVERAGEX`…) or a calculated column, DAX wraps it in an invisible `CALCULATE`. Context transition happens even if you don't write it.

[[diagrama c10-d02]]

## Example

```dax
Ventas = SUM ( fact_ventas[monto_neto] )

Clientes grandes =
COUNTROWS (
    FILTER ( dim_cliente, [Ventas] > 20000000 )
)
```

`FILTER` walks `dim_cliente`. On each row, `[Ventas]` is evaluated as `CALCULATE ( SUM ( fact_ventas[monto_neto] ) )`: it gets filtered by that customer and by whatever the visual already filters, such as the year. If you wrote `SUM ( fact_ventas[monto_neto] )` directly inside the `FILTER`, you'd compare the company total on every row and count everyone or no one.

## In the business

«How many customers buy more than 20 million a year from us?» is a typical sales committee question. This measure answers it by year, region or segment without changing anything.

## Watch out

A measure that «just works» is exactly what's dangerous when you iterate the wrong table. The next lesson shows how a total inflates without warning.
